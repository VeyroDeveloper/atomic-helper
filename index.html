          What does "Atomic" mean?

          The core of your system is one tested image that is updated as a whole, so it can't end up half-updated. Your files, settings and apps live separately and are never touched by system updates.

          Why do I have to restart?

          An update is downloaded and prepared next to your running system. Nothing changes until you restart, and then you boot into the new version. You decide when.

          Something broke after an update. What now?

          Use Roll back on the Home screen, then restart. You can also pick the previous version in the boot menu when your computer starts.

          Where should I install apps from?

          Prefer Flatpak apps (Software or Flathub). They stay separate from the system and update independently. Use system packages only for things that must be part of the system itself.

          Where do I put development tools?

          Use a Toolbx or Distrobox container: a normal Fedora environment where you can install compilers and tools freely without touching the system. Open a terminal and run toolbox create, then toolbox enter.

          What does the Post-install tab do?

          It applies the usual first steps people look up after installing Fedora Atomic: Flathub, RPM Fusion, multimedia codecs and automatic updates. Steps that change the system itself need a restart, and the codecs can only be added once RPM Fusion is active.

          Why am I asked for my password?

          Changing the system is a privileged operation. Your desktop shows a normal password prompt; Atomic Helper never sees or stores your password.
